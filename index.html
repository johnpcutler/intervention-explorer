How to use Intervention Explorer

We spend a lot of time trying to define problems. But sometimes discussing potential actions is the best way to surface our core assumptions and spark the conversations that matter.

Instead of starting with a perfect diagnosis, start by reacting to possible interventions. What resonates? What feels wrong? Your reactions are the diagnosis.

  1. Define what you're seeing. Describe a gap, problem, opportunity, desired intervention, or ideal future state. It doesn't need to be precise — that's what the rest of this process is for.
  2. Browse the interventions. Scan the 102 interventions on the left. Search by keyword, filter by domain or change mechanism. See what catches your eye.
  3. Add promising items to your consideration set. Click "+ Consider" on anything that looks relevant. Don't overthink it — you can always remove items later.
  4. Engage with each consideration. Read the full detail. Notice the assumption it reveals — what are you betting the real problem is? Check the "What if..." challenges. Rate fit (1–5) and add notes about your thinking.
  5. Export and discuss. Copy your consideration set to share with your team. The best insights often come from discussing why certain interventions feel right or wrong.
